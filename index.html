01

What Ovotransferrin (328-332) is

Five amino acids, one lab curiosity — from egg white to test tube.

Ovotransferrin (328-332) is a short synthetic peptide with the amino-acid sequence R V P S L — Arg-Val-Pro-Ser-Leu, abbreviated RVPSL.

It is a fragment of ovotransferrin (also called conalbumin), a major iron-binding protein in egg white. Researchers isolate or synthesize this five-amino-acid piece because it has shown specific biological activities in laboratory studies — most notably blocking an enzyme tied to blood pressure. In vitro

It is sold strictly as a research chemical by specialized peptide suppliers — not a medicine, not a dietary supplement, not approved for any use.

The one-sentence version: RVPSL is a five-letter excerpt from an egg-white protein, studied in labs for its effect on blood-pressure enzymes — a research tool, not a treatment.
02

Where it comes from

Egg white is a library — this peptide is one borrowed page.

Ovotransferrin is one of the main proteins in egg white, where its day job is binding iron — locking the metal away so microbes can't use it. It's a large protein, hundreds of amino acids long.

Positions 328–332 of that chain spell R V P S L. Cut out and synthesized on its own, this fragment behaves differently from the parent protein — a common story in the field of food-derived bioactive peptides, where short pieces of milk, soy, or egg proteins show enzyme effects in lab assays that the whole protein never displays. Conceptual explanation

Why fragments matter: a whole protein is folded, bulky, and busy. A five-amino-acid piece is small enough to fit into enzyme active sites — which is exactly where RVPSL's story begins.

03

The main activity: ACE inhibition

Blocking the enzyme that tightens blood vessels — in a test tube.

ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) is an enzyme that helps raise blood pressure: it converts angiotensin I into the vessel-constricting angiotensin II, and it breaks down bradykinin, a vessel-relaxing signal. Blocking ACE is the mechanism of a whole class of prescription blood-pressure drugs.

RVPSL's headline finding: it inhibits ACE in laboratory assays, with a reported IC50 of approximately 20 μM — the concentration needed to halve the enzyme's activity. That indicates moderate potency in the test tube. In vitro

Keep the scale honest: prescription ACE-inhibitor drugs block ACE at nanomolar concentrations — orders of magnitude lower than 20 micromolar. Moderate test-tube potency is interesting chemistry; it is not a medicine.

What blocking ACE means · the pathway

The mechanism is real chemistry — enzyme blocked, pathway interrupted. Whether that becomes lower blood pressure in a living body is unproven. In vitro

04

The Alzheimer's angle

A second thread of curiosity — not a finding.

Beyond ACE, laboratory work has noted possible activity against enzymes studied in Alzheimer's disease research:

  • Cholinesterase (ChE) inhibition — effects on the enzymes that break down acetylcholine, a neurotransmitter central to memory
  • Possible interactions with BACE1-related pathways — BACE1 (beta-site APP-cleaving enzyme 1) is involved in producing the amyloid fragments studied in Alzheimer's

These observations are early-stage: enzyme-assay hints, not validated effects, and not confirmed in any human trial. In vitro

Read this carefully: "inhibits an enzyme studied in Alzheimer's research" is not "helps Alzheimer's." Dozens of molecules touch these enzymes in test tubes; almost none become brain medicines.

Understanding Ovotransferrin Terminology

Twelve terms, each with its scientific meaning and a plain-English mirror. Tap each term.

Scientific MeaningOvotransferrin (also called conalbumin) is a major iron-binding glycoprotein of egg white, belonging to the transferrin family. Its biological role centers on sequestering iron, limiting microbial growth. It is a large protein of roughly 700 amino acids; the 328–332 fragment (RVPSL) is a short synthetic excerpt studied independently of the parent protein's functions.
Plain EnglishOvotransferrin is one of the main proteins in egg white — its real job is grabbing iron so bacteria can't use it. It's a big protein, about 700 building blocks long. The 328–332 fragment is just five of those blocks, cut out and studied on its own for completely different reasons than the parent protein's iron work.
Scientific MeaningRVPSL is the single-letter amino-acid code for Arg-Val-Pro-Ser-Leu: arginine, valine, proline, serine, leucine. It denotes the exact five-residue sequence of Ovotransferrin (328-332). As a pentapeptide it is small enough to interact directly with enzyme active sites, which underlies its reported ACE-inhibitory activity in vitro.
Plain EnglishRVPSL is shorthand for the peptide's five building blocks: Arginine-Valine-Proline-Serine-Leucine. Five letters, five amino acids — that's the whole molecule. Its small size is the point: it's tiny enough to slot into the working pockets of enzymes, which is how it can block ACE in lab tests.
Scientific MeaningAngiotensin-converting enzyme is a zinc metalloprotease central to blood-pressure regulation. It converts angiotensin I to the vasoconstrictor angiotensin II and degrades bradykinin, a vasodilator — both actions raising blood pressure. ACE inhibitors (e.g., lisinopril, enalapril) are a cornerstone drug class for hypertension and heart failure. RVPSL inhibits ACE in vitro with an IC50 near 20 μM.
Plain EnglishACE is an enzyme that raises blood pressure two ways: it creates a vessel-tightening signal and destroys a vessel-relaxing one. Blocking it is such a good idea that a whole family of blood-pressure pills (like lisinopril) does exactly that. RVPSL also blocks ACE — in a test tube, at moderate strength, which is a long way from being a blood-pressure treatment.
Scientific MeaningIC50 (half-maximal inhibitory concentration) is the concentration of an inhibitor required to reduce an enzyme's activity by 50% under defined assay conditions. Lower IC50 means stronger inhibition. RVPSL's reported IC50 of ~20 μM against ACE indicates moderate potency — genuine activity, but orders of magnitude weaker than pharmaceutical ACE inhibitors, which act in the nanomolar range.
Plain EnglishIC50 is a standard lab measure: how much of a substance does it take to cut an enzyme's activity in half? Lower number = stronger blocker. RVPSL needs about 20 micromolar to halve ACE activity — a respectable middle-of-the-road score, but real blood-pressure drugs do the same job at concentrations thousands of times lower.
Scientific MeaningCholinesterases (acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase) are enzymes that break down the neurotransmitter acetylcholine, terminating its signaling. Inhibiting them prolongs acetylcholine action — the mechanism of several approved Alzheimer's symptom drugs (e.g., donepezil). Laboratory studies have noted cholinesterase-inhibitory effects for RVPSL; these are early in-vitro observations, not therapeutic evidence.
Plain EnglishCholinesterases are enzymes that switch off acetylcholine, a brain chemical important for memory. Blocking them keeps the signal on longer — which is how some approved Alzheimer's drugs work. RVPSL has shown hints of blocking these enzymes in lab dishes. That's an interesting starting clue, not a treatment lead.
Scientific MeaningBACE1 (beta-site APP-cleaving enzyme 1) is the enzyme that makes the first cut in amyloid precursor protein, initiating production of amyloid-beta fragments studied in Alzheimer's disease. It has been a major (and largely disappointing) drug target. Reported interactions of RVPSL with BACE1-related pathways are preliminary laboratory observations with no demonstrated relevance in living systems.
Plain EnglishBACE1 is an enzyme that takes the first snip in creating the amyloid fragments linked to Alzheimer's — drug companies spent billions trying to block it, mostly without success. RVPSL has been noted to interact with BACE1-related pathways in early lab work. Given the graveyard of failed BACE1 drugs, a test-tube hint deserves heavy skepticism, not excitement.
